About

My teaching draws on that practice. I help business students and working professionals understand what AI can and cannot do, how to judge a use case, and how to adopt these tools responsibly. I hold an MBA from the University of North Carolina and a master of engineering degree, and I began my career as a software engineer, so I can move comfortably between the boardroom view of AI and the technical view underneath it.

Educational mindset

I believe learning is a lifelong practice, not a phase that ends with a degree. People learn best when teaching respects the experience they already carry, when hard ideas are made reachable without being made smaller, and when a learner leaves more capable of judging new ideas on their own.

Good teaching also widens the room. When material is designed for many kinds of learners, with plain language, more than one way into a problem, and examples drawn from different lives, everyone learns better, including the learners the material was first built for. Improving learning for everyone is not a slogan in my teaching. It is the design standard.

My story

I came to teaching the long way around, as a student three times over. My first degree was in engineering in India, my first master degree was in engineering in Canada, and after years of industry work I returned to the classroom for an MBA in the United States. Along the way I served three times as a teaching assistant and three times as a research assistant, so I have sat on most sides of a classroom, as the student catching up, the assistant helping others catch up, and the researcher learning how knowledge gets made.

That path is why teaching pulls me. Every room I have learned in contained people at very different starting points, and the teachers I remember are the ones who treated that range as the normal case. I now teach working professionals adopting new technology, community learners meeting it for the first time, and kids meeting it with no fear at all, and the goal is the same in each room. Leave people more confident and more curious than they arrived.

Teaching methodology

  • Begin with a real task from the learner’s world, so every concept has a job to do from the first minute
  • Pair each concept with a worked example, then hand the next example to the learners
  • Build in hands on practice and fast feedback, because skill grows in the attempt, not the lecture
  • Teach limits and judgement alongside capability, so learners know when not to use a tool as well as how
  • Assess by application, through projects, evaluations and short reflections that mirror real decisions
  • Design for every learner in the room, with plain language, multiple entry points and examples from different backgrounds
